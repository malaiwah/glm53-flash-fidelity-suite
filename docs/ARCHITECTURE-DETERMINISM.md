# The same artifact, the same panel, two GPUs, two numbers

The streaming-lane measurement of `turboderp/GLM-5.3-Flash-exl3` @
`51058cd551c7e570d87bd32a4adee720edce2349`, branch 2.05bpw, on brandonmusic's
sealed 25-window panel, was run on three rentals. Two of them agreed **bitwise**
and the third did not:

| provider | GPU | mean_of_run_means_tokenwise_kld | tokenwise-KLD tensor sha256 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vast | A100 80GB **PCIe** (108 SM) | 0.12193502283085089 | `ba59566a3a4911f1adc570a9c74f7dd0731ac32a104a30cc4fb9d1ad473123f6` |
| RunPod | A100-**SXM4**-80GB (108 SM) | 0.12193502283085089 | `ba59566a…` — identical |
| JarvisLabs | H200 (132 SM) | 0.121637676733394**57** | different |

Two providers, two datacenters, two countries, two board variants, two driver
versions (595.84/CUDA 13.2 and 590.48.01/CUDA 13.1), 24 host cores and 256 host
cores — bitwise identical. One different GPU — **2.973e-04 nats apart, 0.245 %
relative**.

This document says where that difference starts, what causes it, which fixes
work, and what the registry should do about it. Every number below is
re-derivable from committed evidence; see [Reproducing this](#reproducing-this).

---

## 1. Summary

* **The divergence is upstream of the KLD estimator.** The student forward
  produces different logits on different GPUs. The estimator contributes a
  second, much smaller divergence of its own, and that second one **is** cheaply
  fixable.
* **It is not "Ampere vs Hopper".** Eight GPU models were probed. On a full
  fp32 transformer forward from identical weights and identical inputs, **all
  eight produced a distinct result** — including two cards that share a compute
  capability. **No** divergent quantity in the whole battery is a function of
  compute capability alone.
* **It is the GPU model, not the machine.** Every card was rented twice, on
  different physical hosts; three of the eight came back on a *different driver
  version*. Every GPU-side digest reproduced bit for bit. The only quantity that
  moved between rentals was the CPU-side control.
* **TF32 is not the cause** (it is already off, and the TF32-off path diverges),
  and `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(True)` plus
  `CUBLAS_WORKSPACE_CONFIG=:4096:8` changed **nothing at all** — not one digest
  within a box, and not one agreement across boxes.
* **Verdict: NOT FIXABLE in practice for the forward.** The only lever left is
  an fp64 or fixed-order reference GEMM, at 16–64× the arithmetic cost.
* **Verdict: FIXABLE cheaply for the estimator.** Raising the scorer's
  `--chunk-positions` from 512 to ≥ 640 makes `_token_kld` bitwise identical on
  every card tested. It is a protocol change, not a bug fix, because it moves
  the published digest.
* **Registry consequence:** the GPU model does **not** belong in
  `COMPARABILITY_KEY_FIELDS` — it would regroup all 75 published rows into
  mostly-singletons and cannot even be computed for 13 of the 15 multi-row
  groups. What belongs there is a **recorded hardware identity, a disclosure,
  and a rankability rule**. See §7.

## 2. What was measured, and how

`reports/arch-determinism/probe_payload.py` runs on a rented box and computes a
layered battery whose every input is generated by numpy's PCG64 on the CPU and
hashed, so "both boxes were fed the same bytes" is evidence rather than an
assumption. Ten layers, cheapest first, so the divergence can be **localised**
rather than guessed at:

| layer | what it computes |
|---|---|
| L1 | `exp`/`log`/`tanh`/`rsqrt`/`erf`/`sigmoid`, fp32 and fp64 |
| L2 | GEMM in bf16/fp16/fp32(TF32 off)/fp32(TF32 on)/fp64, at a square shape, the streaming lane's own skinny expert shape (2047×4096×2048) and a split-k-bait shape (256×16384×256) |
| L3 | `torch.nn.functional.grouped_mm` — the MoE kernel the streaming lane really dispatches |
| L4 | `sum`/`mean`/`logsumexp` over dim −1 and dim 0, fp32 and fp64, at 8192 and at the real 154880-wide vocabulary |
| L5 | `softmax` / `log_softmax`, fp32 and fp64, at the real vocabulary |
| L6 | `engines/tools/k6_kld_report.py::_token_kld` **verbatim**, on synthetic logits at the real vocabulary |
| L7 | a fixed-weight 16-layer decoder forward, bf16 and fp32, hidden states kept at every depth |
| L8 | the fp64 last-dim sum at 11 row counts, 16384 columns |
| L9 | the same sweep at 154880 columns |
| L10 | `_token_kld` verbatim at position blocks 256 / 512 / 640 / 1024 |

Everything runs **twice per box, as two processes**: once under the streaming
lane's own numeric policy, and once with `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(True)`
and `CUBLAS_WORKSPACE_CONFIG=:4096:8` — set in the environment before the
cuBLAS handle exists, because a flag flipped mid-process is too late to change
the workspace the handle already took.

## 3. Where the divergence starts

With the software stack held constant and the inputs byte-identical, 53 of 111
result keys differ across the eight cards. By layer:

| layer | keys | variant | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 elementwise / transcendental | 12 | **0** | exonerated |
| L2 GEMM | 15 | **13** | **the source** |
| L3 grouped GEMM | 1 | **0** | exonerated *at this shape* |
| L4 reductions | 12 | **4** | only the wide (real-vocabulary) and whole-tensor cases |
| L5 softmax / log_softmax | 4 | **0** | exonerated |
| L6 the scorer's estimator @512 | 8 | **2** | a second, independent source |
| L7 transformer forward | 37 | **34** | the consequence |
| L8 reduction sweep @16384 cols | 22 | **0** | exonerated at that width |

The shallowest divergent quantity is a **matrix multiply**. `L2.expert.bf16` —
2047×4096×2048 in bf16, exactly the streaming lane's expert-GEMM shape — gives
four different answers across eight cards from the same two input tensors.

Two GEMM results are invariant on all eight cards: `L2.sq2048.fp64` and
`L2.expert.fp64`. That matters for §6.

**And it is not TF32.** The lane already sets
`torch.backends.cuda.matmul.allow_tf32 = False`,
`torch.backends.cudnn.allow_tf32 = False` and
`torch.set_float32_matmul_precision("highest")`
(`engines/tools/stream_score.py:350–355`), the on-box `backend.json` records
`numeric_policy.allow_tf32_cuda_matmul: false` as *observed*, and the probe
re-checks it. `L2.expert.fp32_tf32off` diverges into three groups anyway.
Turning TF32 on changes the values; it does not change whether the cards agree.

### The teacher is not the source — verified, not assumed

All three rentals of the real measurement resolved the **same** teacher and the
**same** panel, by digest:

```
teacher_receipt_sha256      2ae08117c3d4247f747b2a9a889b68e1a06387b788d56a0bf23bb950c77bc5a5
token_panel_receipt_sha256  0beec5770e5107547731b084f1bc5f9fb8ba79d67af56ddb70d919da367737d5
teacher_backend_identity    85b11599c6b36a83fa8099a09a298a386a0c603d1f18d3702e7fb1c470962ce4
```

and the per-window `token_ids_sha256` / `attention_mask_sha256` sets are
identical across all 25 windows. The teacher is a fetched, sealed panel and it
was the same bytes on every box.

### The student logits moved — proved from the published receipts alone

`per_run_top1_agreement` is 0.8876990718124084 on A100 and 0.8891841719589644 on
H200: **45,428 versus 45,504 argmax matches out of 51,175 positions**. Seventy-six
positions changed which token the student ranks first.

`argmax` does not care about summation order, so a reduction-order difference
inside the estimator cannot produce that. Only different **student logits** can.
The per-window means agree: 18 of 25 windows differ by more than 1e-3, the
per-window deltas are sign-random (14 positive, 11 negative, sd 2.92e-3, range
−5.44e-3 … +5.79e-3), and per-window *maxima* move a lot (window `final-0000`:
3.78 on H200, 4.44 on A100 — a single heavy-tail position).

**The 2.973e-04 panel figure is the residual of a ±5e-3 per-window
disagreement that mostly cancels over 25 windows.** Anyone comparing on fewer
windows is exposed to far more than 2.973e-04.

## 4. What explains the grouping

For every divergent key the partition of cards is tested against the two
candidate explanations. Of 53 variant keys:

* **0 are a function of compute capability alone.**
* 18 are a function of SM count alone — but see the caveat below.
* 18 put every card in its own group.

The headline case:

```
L7.fp32.logits        8 groups — every card its own
    ada-l4(sm_89,58SM)
    ada-l40s(sm_89,142SM)
    ampere-a100-sxm4-80gb(sm_80,108SM)
    ampere-a6000(sm_86,84SM)
    blackwell-pro4000(sm_120,70SM)
    blackwell-pro6000(sm_120,188SM)
    hopper-h100-pcie(sm_90,114SM)
    hopper-h100-sxm(sm_90,132SM)
```

Because the eight cards happen to have eight distinct SM counts, an
all-singletons partition is *trivially* a function of SM count. That is not
evidence for SM count; it is evidence that there is nothing coarser to group by.
The non-vacuous test is a partition that is neither all-singletons nor
all-one-group, and those are explained by **neither** attribute:

```
L7.bf16.logits        6 groups — cap-explained=False, sm-explained=False
    ada-l4 | ada-l40s | ampere-a100-sxm4-80gb | ampere-a6000
    blackwell-pro4000, hopper-h100-pcie, hopper-h100-sxm
    blackwell-pro6000

L2.sq2048.bf16        3 groups — cap-explained=False, sm-explained=False
    ada-l4(sm_89), ampere-a6000(sm_86)
    ada-l40s(sm_89), ampere-a100-sxm4-80gb(sm_80)
    blackwell-pro4000(sm_120), blackwell-pro6000(sm_120),
        hopper-h100-pcie(sm_90), hopper-h100-sxm(sm_90)
```

Read that middle block: on a square bf16 GEMM, an L40S (Ada) agrees with an
A100 (Ampere) while disagreeing with an L4 that shares its own compute
capability; and Hopper agrees with Blackwell. On the *skinny expert* shape the
same eight cards regroup differently again (`L2.expert.bf16`, 4 groups).

So the three hypotheses the coordinator posed resolve as:

* **"Blackwell == Ampere, Hopper is the outlier"** — false. Blackwell and Ampere
  are in different groups on every headline key, and Hopper is frequently
  grouped *with* Blackwell.
* **"They cluster by tensor-core generation"** — false as stated. Clusters exist
  but they cross generations and change with the GEMM shape.
* **"Every architecture differs, there is no correct value to converge on"** —
  **this one, and stronger than asked**: at the level of a whole model forward
  it is every *card*, not every architecture.

The mechanism is that cuBLASLt/CUTLASS selects a tile shape, an instruction
shape and a split-k factor per (architecture, SM count, problem shape). Those
choices change the **order** of a floating-point accumulation chain, and
floating-point addition is not associative. Nothing here is a bug in any layer
of the stack; it is the intended behaviour of a heuristic kernel selector.

### The one place the mechanism is exactly pinned

The estimator's reduction is simpler than a GEMM and its threshold can be
located precisely. ATen chooses a CUDA reduction configuration from *device
properties*, and when the number of output rows is small relative to what the
device can occupy it splits each row's sum across several blocks and combines
them — a different addition order.

`sum(dim=-1)` in fp64, same input bytes, seven cards:

| columns | rows 16–512 | rows 640 | rows 768 | rows 1024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16,384 | 1 group throughout | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| **154,880** | **2–3 groups at every row count** | **1** | **1** | **1** |

At a moderate reduction length the split heuristic has nothing to win and every
card does the same in-order sum. At the real 154,880-wide vocabulary the cards
disagree at every row count up to and including 512, and agree from 640 up.

Running `_token_kld` **verbatim** confirms it end-to-end:

| `--chunk-positions` | digest groups across 7 cards |
|---|---|
| 256 | 3 |
| **512  ← what the streaming lane runs** | **2** — `{blackwell-pro6000, hopper-h100-sxm}` against the rest |
| **640** | **1** |
| 1024 | **1** |

`kld_report.py`'s own default is 16 (`--chunk-positions`, line 685), which is
deeper inside the split regime still.

**How much of the 2.973e-04 is this?** Almost none. At chunk 512 the two groups'
tokenwise values differ by `max_abs = 9.714e-17` on values of order 1e-2 —
about 1e-14 relative — and the printed means are identical to 18 significant
digits. The estimator breaks the **digest**, which is the suite's determinism
evidence; the **number** is moved by the forward.

## 5. Does forcing determinism help? No — and that is a measurement

Both processes per box, on all eight cards:

```
torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(True)  +  CUBLAS_WORKSPACE_CONFIG=:4096:8

  keys changed WITHIN a box   0 of 111    (on every one of the eight cards)
  keys still differing ACROSS boxes   53   (all of them)
```

Zero within-box change is itself informative: the lane was **already** running
the deterministic kernels. These settings buy determinism on one device across
runs, which this suite already had (`bitwise_deterministic: true`, one distinct
tokenwise digest per machine, two cold runs). They buy **nothing** across
devices, because they do not change which kernel the heuristic picks — only
whether that kernel is itself run-to-run stable.

## 6. Verdict on fixability

### The estimator: FIXABLE cheaply, but it is a protocol change

Raise `--chunk-positions` to ≥ 640. Proven above: 7 of 7 cards produce a bitwise
identical tokenwise KLD at 640 and at 1024, and do not at 512 or 256. The cost
is memory, not time: 640 × 154,880 in fp64 is 793 MB per buffer against 634 MB
at 512, and the estimator holds a handful of them — the receipts show the whole
25-window fp64 scoring stage takes 57 s on an A100.

It is **not** a free bug fix, and it must not be retrofitted quietly:

* the block size changes the tokenwise values bitwise (the code says so itself,
  NUM-09, and refuses a resume across a block-size change);
* so it changes `tokenwise_kld_sha256`, which is the registry's determinism
  evidence, and moves published numbers in their last bits;
* and the threshold is **device-derived**, so 640 is a value verified on eight
  specific cards, not a proof. A future card with more SMs than 188 could push
  the threshold higher. The rule to adopt is *verify the threshold on the cards
  you use*, with `L9`/`L10` as the check — not *640 is safe*.

Recommended, not implemented here: this belongs in a numbered protocol revision
with the delta disclosed, not in a patch to a live campaign's runner.

### The forward: NOT FIXABLE in practice

Eight cards, identical weights, identical inputs, identical software, TF32 off,
deterministic algorithms on — eight distinct fp32 logit tensors. There is no
setting that makes them agree because there is no shared kernel to agree on.

The remaining levers, priced:

| lever | does it work? | cost |
|---|---|---|
| TF32 off | already on; does not help | 0 |
| `use_deterministic_algorithms(True)` + `CUBLAS_WORKSPACE_CONFIG` | **measured: does not help** | 0 |
| pin the position block ≥ 640 | fixes the estimator only | ~0 |
| **fp64 forward** | probably: `L2.sq2048.fp64` and `L2.expert.fp64` are invariant on all 8 cards — but `L2.splitk.fp64` gives **6 groups**, so fp64 alone is not a guarantee; the algorithm would have to be pinned too | A100 bf16 tensor 312 TFLOP/s vs fp64 tensor 19.5 → **16×**; on RTX PRO 6000 / L40S / L4, fp64 runs at 1/64 of fp32 → **~100×+**. A 3-hour measurement becomes days to weeks, and the cheap cards leave the fleet entirely |
| a hand-written fixed-order reference GEMM | yes by construction | a new kernel to write, prove bitwise against the ecosystem reference (this suite's own bar), and maintain; orders of magnitude slower than a tuned tensor-core GEMM |

Both of the last two also **change the measurement**: an fp64 forward is a
different lane, so its numbers would not be comparable with the 75 rows already
published. That is the honest summary — the cure creates a new comparability
group of its own.

**So: NOT FIXABLE.** The consequence therefore matters more than the cause.

## 7. What the registry should do

### 7.1 Practical significance: can hardware swamp the artifact differences we publish?

**Yes, at 4 bpw and above.**

The registry publishes, in one comparability group and one lane:

```
measurement--glm53.tr3-4bpw-stream.brandonmusic-final25       0.025503427634363770
measurement--glm53.turbo-4.05bpw-stream.brandonmusic-final25  0.025526426915472484
                                                    difference 2.300e-05
```

The measured hardware term between an A100 and an H200 on this panel is
**2.973e-04 nats**, which is **13× that difference**. If the term is instead
taken as proportional (0.245 % of the measured value), it is 6.25e-05 at
KLD 0.0255 — still **2.7×**. Under either scaling, hardware alone swamps the
gap this registry currently reports as separating two 4-bit quantizers.

The published pair is *not* invalidated: both rows were measured on the **same**
H200 (`pipeline--malaiwah.glm53-stream-packed-kld`, `hardware.gpu: "H200"`), so
the comparison is same-hardware and the hardware term largely cancels. The
hazard is prospective — the next contributor's A100 row lands in that same
comparability group and is rendered in the same ranked table.

**Where it starts to matter.** Take the term as ~0.25 % of the measured KLD.
Two rows measured on different GPU models are separable only if they differ by
more than that:

| pair | difference | 0.25 % of value | separable across hardware? |
|---|---|---|---|
| K6 6bpw vs K8 8bpw | 1.33e-03 (10.6 %) | ~3e-05 | **yes**, comfortably |
| dione-3.0bpw vs turbo-4.05bpw | 2.50e-02 (98 %) | ~1e-04 | **yes** |
| tr3-4bpw vs turbo-4.05bpw | 2.30e-05 (0.09 %) | 6.3e-05 | **no** |

The threshold is not a bit rate, it is a *closeness*: it bites wherever two
artifacts land within ~0.25 % of each other, which in this registry happens
among the good quants (4 bpw and up), not among the aggressive ones.

### 7.2 Does the GPU belong in `COMPARABILITY_KEY_FIELDS`? — No. Here is why, and what instead

The tempting move is to add hardware to `COMPARABILITY_KEY_FIELDS`
(`registry/tools/registry_lib.py:82`) the way `panel_id` and `reference_id` are
there, following the REFC-006 / `DESIGNATED-REFERENCE.md` pattern. **Do not**,
for three reasons that the evidence above forces:

1. **The right granularity is the GPU model, and that fragments everything.**
   The evidence is not "architectures differ" — it is that all eight cards
   differ on a full forward. A key field coarser than the model (SM major, or
   "Ampere/Hopper/Blackwell") would be *wrong*: it would license ranking an L4
   against an L40S, which this document shows produce different numbers. A key
   field at model granularity turns most groups into singletons, and
   `registry_render.py` already prints "nothing to rank against" for those. The
   registry would stop ranking anything.

2. **It cannot be computed for the rows that already exist.** Of the 15
   comparability groups with more than one row, **13** contain at least one row
   whose `hardware.gpu` is `"cuda"`, `null`, or a capability string (`"SM120"`)
   rather than a GPU model. Only two groups could produce a
   model-granularity key for every row. The key is a hash over its inputs; an
   input that is `null` for two thirds of the corpus does not make a key, it
   makes a second `null` bucket that means "unknown" and reads as "same".

3. **Changing the key regroups every published measurement.** The validator
   recomputes the key from a row's own authoritative fields and compares it with
   the stored block (`key_inputs_from_measurement`), so a new field invalidates
   all 75 stored keys at once, re-splits every rendered table, and breaks every
   external citation of a `cmp--…` id. That cost is only worth paying for an
   invariant that actually separates comparable from incomparable, and per (1)
   this one does not.

**Recommended instead — three changes, in increasing cost:**

**(a) Record the hardware, at model granularity, mechanically.** Extend
`pipeline.schema.json`'s `hardware` block from `{gpu, gpu_count,
tensor_parallel, note}` to also carry `gpu_model` (the exact
`torch.cuda.get_device_name(0)` string), `compute_capability` and
`multi_processor_count`, and have the receipt builder fill them from
`backend.json`'s `lane_identity` — which **already records `device_name`** and
already hashes it into `lane_identity_sha256`. Nothing is invented; the streaming
lane has been carrying this all along and the registry has been dropping it.
`"cuda"` as a value of `gpu` should become a validation *warning*: it is the
absence of the field wearing a plausible name.

**(b) A new invariant, `HW-001`, at error level.** Two clauses, both of which
this document's evidence justifies:

  1. A measurement whose `determinism.evidence_kind` is a tensor digest and
     whose `distinct_evidence_hash_count` is 1 must name its `gpu_model`. A
     bitwise-determinism claim that does not say *on what* is not checkable, and
     — per §3 — is silently a claim about one card.
  2. Rows sharing a comparability key that name **different** `gpu_model`s must
     each carry a `cross_hardware` disclosure with `affects_comparability: true`,
     and the renderer must annotate that group. This is the same shape as the
     `different_reference_kind` disclosure REFC-006 requires, and for the same
     reason: the key cannot stop a reader from ranking two rows, so the row has
     to say so out loud.

**(c) Make the term available for arithmetic.** `bin/fidelity-stats
paired-delta` already refuses cross-teacher and cross-panel pairs; it should
refuse — or at minimum flag with the measured term — a cross-`gpu_model` pair,
exactly as `BIAS-006` refuses a cross-lane floor subtraction. The measured
hardware term for this panel and lane, 2.973e-04 nats between A100 (108 SM) and
H200 (132 SM), is the first entry in that table.

**Migration consequences of (a)–(c), stated plainly:**

* No comparability key changes, so **no published row is regrouped** and no
  `cmp--…` id moves. This is the whole point of preferring disclosure to keying.
* Clause 1 of `HW-001` fails **immediately** for every existing row that claims
  bitwise determinism without naming a GPU. The honest options are (i) backfill
  from the source receipts where they exist — the streaming-lane rows already
  name `H200` at pipeline level, so those pass — and (ii) grandfather the rest
  by version: apply `HW-001` to `schema_version` ≥ the revision that introduces
  it. Grandfathering is what the suite did for `position_block` (NUM-09 treats a
  missing value as *unknown*, and only an explicit mismatch refuses); the same
  precedent applies.
* Clause 2 changes rendering for any group that mixes hardware. Today that is
  three groups, all of which mix a declared `H200`/`SM120` with `null` — i.e.
  they are *already* mixing and nobody can currently see it.
* **Nothing here should be done unilaterally.** Changing an invariant changes
  what the registry accepts from third parties; this is a proposal.

### 7.3 The one thing that should change immediately, and costs nothing

`bitwise_deterministic: true` and `distinct_tokenwise_kld_sha256` with one entry
are true and useful statements — **about one machine**. The `cold_run_deviation`
field already carries free-text disclosure ("2 cold runs, not 5 (budget;
disclosed)"). Determinism notes should say which GPU the runs were on. That is a
documentation change, not a schema change, and it removes the only reading of
those fields that is actually misleading.

## 8. Does the magnitude scale with the model?

The probe's 16-layer toy decoder gives the small-model end of the answer. With
one card's fp32 logits nominated as the panel and each card's bf16 forward as
the student, scored on the CPU in fp64 with identical arithmetic for all:


Set beside the real artifact:

| | model | KLD | hardware spread, absolute | relative |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| toy | 16 layers, hidden 1024, vocab 32768 | 6.3e-05 | **3.49e-06** | 5.5 % |
| GLM-5.3-Flash 2.05bpw | 45 layers, vocab 154880 | 0.1219 | **2.97e-04** | 0.245 % |

**In absolute nats the term grows with the model — 85× from the toy to the
production model.** So 2.973e-04 is representative of a production-scale
measurement, not a coincidence of one artifact.

In *relative* terms it moves the other way, and for a reason worth stating: the
toy's KLD has no quantization in it at all — it is a bf16-versus-fp32 rounding
floor — so the denominator is tiny. The numerator, not the ratio, is the
transferable quantity.

The depth mechanism is visible directly. Maximum absolute difference in the
bf16 hidden state against the reference card, by layer:

```
L40S vs L4, max |Δ| in the bf16 hidden state
layer      1      2      3      4      5      6      7      8
        1.6e-2 4.7e-2 6.2e-2 9.4e-2 9.4e-2 1.2e-1 1.6e-1 1.9e-1
layer      9     10     11     12     13     14     15     16
        1.9e-1 2.5e-1 2.5e-1 2.5e-1 2.5e-1 3.1e-1 3.1e-1 3.8e-1
```

A single bf16 rounding step at layer 1 becomes ~24× that by layer 16 — growth
that is steady rather than explosive, so a 45-layer model plausibly carries a
few times the layer-16 figure. (That last step is an extrapolation from a toy;
the 2.973e-04 figure is measured, this is not.) At the output, **84.3–84.7 % of
all logits differ**, by a median fp32-ULP distance of 196,608 — exactly 3 bf16
steps.

That is the whole causal chain, end to end: one GEMM tile ordering → a few ULPs
in one hidden state → 84 % of logits differ → 76 argmax flips in 51,175
positions → ±5e-3 per window → 2.973e-04 on the panel mean.
